# The look

The rules for how the app looks: colors and contrast, the feature colors, corners and edges, and motion. Part of the rules for the code; [`README.md`](./README.md) lists the others. How the look came about is in the topic files under `docs/ui/`.

## Colors and contrast

- **Colors come from the theme**, `assets/css/theme.css`: Catppuccin Latte in light mode and Catppuccin Mocha in dark mode, taken from `@catppuccin/palette`. Every token is a `light-dark()` of the two, so the device's setting picks (`color-scheme: light dark`; Quasar follows with `dark: 'auto'`). The production build rewrites `light-dark()` for older browsers; `nuxt dev` and `public/offline.html` need Chrome 123, Firefox 120 or Safari 17.5. `light-dark()` takes colors only, so a value that differs by mode has to be part of a color (the veil's strength is its alpha). Never write a color value or a fixed Quasar palette name (`grey-8`, `red-9`) in a component: use a token (`var(--theme-surface)`, `--theme-text-muted`, `--theme-outline`), a brand name (`color="primary"`, `text-negative`) or one of the theme's own names (`text-muted`, `text-on-accent`, `ink-red`). A value is the package's own, or a `color-mix()` of the package's values where a token needs a shade Catppuccin doesn't have (the strong shades below). `public/offline.html` is the one place with the values written out; `nuxt.config.ts` and `scripts/generate-pwa-icons.mjs` (the favicon and the app icons in `public/`) read them from the package.
- **Text on the primary and the negative color is `--theme-on-accent`**, not white: Mocha's accents are pastel, and white on them is at about 2:1. `theme.css` gives it to filled controls and the header. Catppuccin's pairings don't guarantee 4.5:1 (Latte: base on mauve is 4.8:1, and of the accents only mauve and red carry it at all), so calculate a new pair; `a11y.e2e.ts` scans every page in both modes.
- **A link inside a list is in the text's color with an underline in `--theme-outline`** (`text-decoration-color`, `text-underline-offset: 3px`), never the browser's link blue, which is no token and is not checked in dark mode. Text that is not a link has no underline.
- **A row is marked by a tint of the primary color at 8% over the page**: the text and `text-muted` on it stay above 4.5:1 in both modes, the primary color as text does not (4.28:1 in light mode). Never a colored edge on one side of a row or a card.
- **Something not planned yet has a dashed `--theme-outline` edge**, and nothing else has a dashed edge.
- **Hover and keyboard focus move a control on an accent color away from its text**, where Quasar would move it towards it: `assets/css/hover-contrast.css` lays the theme's veil over it (`--theme-veil`: black at 30% in light mode, white at 65% in dark mode, where a veil shows less). The header's own highlights (wordmark, account button) use the same token. A flat button without a color of its own gets `--theme-veil-on-surface`, in the text's color and only as strong as leaves its text above 4.5:1. Don't give a flat button a color (`flat` with `color="primary"`): no highlight leaves colored text on a surface above 4.5:1 in light mode. Quasar's dialog plugin does it by default; pass `color: undefined` for its flat button.
- **A whiteboard stroke is stored with its pen's fixed `#rrggbb` and drawn in the theme's color for that pen** (`utils/ink.ts`, `--ink-*` in `theme.css`), so the board is light or dark with the rest. The avatars' rosettes are drawn the same way.
- **Every pair of text and background is at 4.5:1 or more, every icon or edge that carries meaning at 3:1 or more**, in both modes. `a11y.e2e.ts` scans every page in both; `utils/theme-tokens.spec.ts` calculates the pairs the scan can't see (a hovered button on a tint), from the package's values and the figures in `theme.css`.
- **The browser's own surfaces are the theme's**: selected text is the text's color on `--theme-selection`, and the caret, the scrollbars and native controls (`accent-color`) are set on the root in `theme.css`.

## Feature colors

- **Each feature has one color**, the same on its dashboard tile, its page's heading and its empty state: to-dos blue, shopping green, meals and the Tonight tile peach, recipes yellow, vacations teal, whiteboard pink, the household and the kids mauve. The primary color stays mauve everywhere: filled buttons, the header, focus outlines and spinners don't take a feature's color.
- **Three tokens per feature** in `theme.css`: `--feature-<name>` (the accent: a pen stroke, nothing that must stand out from its background, since Latte's pastels are below 3:1), `--feature-<name>-tint` (a background, as strong as leaves the text, the muted text, a hovered flat button and red text at 4.5:1 or more on it) and `--feature-<name>-strong` (an icon or an edge, at 3:1 or more on the page, a surface and its own tint). A feature's color is never text.
- **A strong shade exists only where the browser has `color-mix()`.** In light mode most of them are a mix of the accent with Latte's text, which the build leaves to the browser. So every use names what an older browser gets instead: `var(--feature-<name>-strong, var(--theme-outline))` for an edge or an outline, `var(--feature-<name>-strong, var(--theme-text))` for an icon.
- **A fourth token, `--feature-<name>-edge`, is a card's edge**: the accent at the least alpha (steps of 5%, per ground) that stands out from the page and a surface as much as `--theme-separator`, and below 3:1, so it stays faint. A feature's edge is never the strong shade except where it has to lead (the Tonight tile's 2px).
- **A feature's color goes through `featureColors()` (`utils/features.ts`) and the shared components**: `<pen-stroke>` for a stroke under a heading, `<page-heading>` for a page's heading with its icon, `<empty-state>` for a drawing and its text (all in `components/ui/`). They name the fallbacks once; a component doesn't write `--feature-*` names or a fallback itself. A new feature is added to `FEATURE_ICONS` and `theme.css` together; `features.spec.ts` checks that they agree.
- **SVG decoration names its forced-colors color.** Forced colors leave an SVG's strokes alone in Chrome, so a drawing sets `CanvasText` (a link-like stroke `LinkText`) inside `@media (forced-colors: active)`.
- **A new tint, shade or pair is calculated before it is used**: add it to `utils/theme-tokens.spec.ts`, which computes the blend and the mix as the browser does (`tests/support/color-contrast.ts`).

## Corners and edges

- **Corners follow the radius scale, by size**: `--radius-field` (0.5rem) for fields and small boxes inside a card, `--radius-control` (0.625rem) for buttons and controls, `--radius-menu` (0.75rem) for menus, tooltips, notices and the date picker, `--radius-card` (1rem) for cards, tiles and bordered lists, `--radius-dialog` (1.25rem) for dialogs. In `rem`, so they grow with the reader's text size. Round things (avatars, round buttons, the tick) stay round. Never a radius in pixels: a highlight drawn along a corner of its own (a tile's stretched link, the wordmark) takes the token of what it surrounds, and `a11y.e2e.ts` checks those two.
- **Quasar's corners are overridden in `theme.css`**, for the components the app uses (outlined fields, buttons, menus, tooltips, notices, the date picker, cards, dialogs). A Quasar component used for the first time keeps its 3 or 4px until it is added there.
- **A card takes its edge from `--card-edge` and `--card-edge-width`** (fallback `--theme-separator` and 1px) in the `.q-card` rule of `theme.css`; a feature's card sets the two properties instead of fighting that rule's specificity.
- **A card has a 1px edge in `--theme-separator` and no shadow; what floats above the page has a shadow and no edge**: dialogs (their card is exempt), menus and notices. A shadow means "above the page", an edge "a card".

## Motion

- **Every animation is inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, every hover effect inside `@media (any-hover: hover)`.** Nothing loops and nothing starts without the person doing something.
- **Transforms and opacity only, 120 to 250ms, ease-out**, so nothing shifts the layout. The tick's single overshoot is the one exception and the app's only bounce. The drawn strike-through is the one animation of something else: a background's size on the title's inline span, which strikes every line of a title that wraps and only repaints.
- **The shared motion is in `assets/css/motion.css`**: a pressed button gives way (0.97), the rows of a list, and the tick. A component with motion of its own (a tile's lift and press, `DashboardTile.vue`) keeps it in its own style, under the same guards. Quasar's own motion (the ripple, the checkbox's fill and tick) is switched off there under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
- **A list's rows move in a `<transition-group name="list-row">` without a `tag`**, so the rows stay the `q-list`'s own children (its separators and its role), on a `q-list` with the class `list-rows`. A leaving row is taken out of the flow and the rows below slide up by transform; a row that changes its place (a ticked shopping item) slides there. The group's `@before-leave` is `makeLeavingRowInert` (`utils/list-motion.ts`): a leaving row can't be pressed, focused or read. A leave hook takes one argument only, or Vue waits for it instead of the transition.
- **A change from another member moves rows, nothing more.** Rows slide in and collapse for every change; anything that answers a tap (the tick's overshoot and drawn strike-through, a celebration) is started from the tap's handler in the component, never from a store's state, so a Realtime change can't set it off. The tick goes through `useTickDrawing()`.
- **The e2e suite runs without motion**; how a spec turns it on, and how the helpers treat a leaving row, is in "What the tests need" in [`commands.md`](./commands.md).
